costs callsLearn whether Buy‑It‑Now listings ever sell

The tool is blind to 94% of the market on the outcome side. A Buy‑It‑Now listing is Good‑’Til‑Cancelled, carries no end time, and the resolution queue only accepts listings whose end time has passed — so a BIN lot can never enter it. Every completed outcome in the store is an auction.

It would not give you BIN clearing prices. The tool refuses those by design: a Buy‑It‑Now sale says what one buyer would pay on demand, not where the market clears. What it gives you is BIN sell‑through — currently unknown, and arguably the more useful number. Knowing that shelf sovereigns at 40% over melt essentially never sell tells you what to ignore.

The naive trigger is a trap: most apparent departures are eBay rotating which of a dealer’s identical variation listings it surfaces. 73.6% of departed listings share a title with another listing, and every one of those twins is the same seller.

  3. Trigger on four consecutive absent sweeps, gated to classified listings with no same‑seller twin.
  4. Split sell‑through by format before writing any BIN outcome — folding it into the existing column would pollute a number that is auction‑derived today.

Kept here because each one cost real data before it was found, and the shape of the mistake tends to recur.

Punctuation no longer hides a denomination

Fixed three times by widening a list of allowed characters — brackets, then commas, then asterisks and hashes. *HALF* SOVEREIGN was priced against a full sovereign’s gold and showed a 19% edge that did not exist. Punctuation is now stripped before the denomination is read, which ends the whole class.

Country filtering is opt‑in, and defaults to the UK

Screening to GB alone removed 1,268 genuine sovereigns in one pass, 744 of them Australian — Sydney, Melbourne and Perth mint coins are British sovereigns and the scarcest part of the series. The filter is a multi‑select on the market page with the cost of narrowing shown beside each country.

Two phrases were eating the best data in the store

Of 23 completed sales only 14 were counted. Three sovereigns that fetched £809, £829 and £861 said “Never Cleaned Or Mounted” and were dropped as jewellery; two more said “in Capsule”. Negations and packaging are stripped before the rules run.

A stray click could have excluded 414 sovereigns

Accepting a learned rule was one click, and george would have stopped pricing 414 lots. Rules that break nothing keep their button; anything that would remove priced coins goes behind a confirmation page that names them.
